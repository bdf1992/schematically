# Research — infinite canvas rendering + simulation, 2026 state of the art

Answers the two research questions left open in `HANDOFF.md`. Run 2026-09-01 with WebSearch/WebFetch.
The `WebSearch` 400 that stopped the previous session was a harness config in that session, not a tool defect.

## A — Rendering

### Figma

Figma's renderer is a C++ core compiled to WebAssembly driving a custom tile-based GPU renderer.
They deliberately did not use HTML, SVG or Canvas — inconsistent text layout and missing features.
The compiled core buys them control of memory layout (32-bit floats, pre-allocated, no GC pauses).
In September 2025 they migrated the renderer from WebGL to WebGPU: explicit draw-call arguments,
a custom GLSL→WGSL shader processor so one shader source serves both APIs, batched uniform uploads.
Result reported as "a performance improvement on some classes of devices, neutral on others, no regressions."
They kept a dynamic runtime fallback to WebGL on device loss. Compute shaders for blur, MSAA and
RenderBundles are named as the next wins — things WebGL cannot do. No public detail on their LOD scheme.

- https://www.figma.com/blog/building-a-professional-design-tool-on-the-web/
- https://www.figma.com/blog/figma-rendering-powered-by-webgpu/

### tldraw / Excalidraw — the DOM class ceiling

tldraw renders shapes as DOM/SVG and survives by culling: an R-tree spatial index picks visible shapes,
everything else gets `display:none`. Their own example is 10,000 shapes on a page with 50 rendered.
`maxShapesPerPage` defaults to 4,000 (docs elsewhere cite 10,000 as the raised value); a
`debouncedZoomThreshold` of 500 shapes switches zoom to debounced above that count.
Excalidraw uses a dual-canvas split (elements vs. interactive overlay) with viewport culling and
throttling; users report it performing well at 4k–8k elements in the viewport and struggling past 8k.

Read against our benchmark: our SVG surface falling over at ~500 components / 18.6k elements is normal
for this class, and the whole class tops out three orders of magnitude below the 1.4M-port bar.

- https://tldraw.dev/sdk-features/performance
- https://tldraw.dev/sdk-features/culling
- https://deepwiki.com/tldraw/tldraw/3.4-shape-rendering-and-culling
- https://github.com/excalidraw/excalidraw/issues/8136

### Vello

Alpha. Three implementations now: GPU-compute Vello, Vello CPU, and Vello Hybrid. December 2025 brought
a 30% gain from new overdraw handling plus stroke/gradient fixes. `bevy_vello` tracks Bevy 0.17.
Their own docs say WebGPU support in Firefox and Safari is still experimental and they test on Chrome.
Not a dependency to build a product bar on this year.

- https://linebender.org/blog/tmil-24/
- https://github.com/linebender/vello
- https://docs.rs/vello_hybrid

### Large-graph renderers — the closest existing proof

cosmos.gl (formerly Cosmograph, now an OpenJS Foundation project) runs both the force-layout simulation
and the drawing entirely in vertex/fragment shaders so data never leaves the GPU, and claims real-time
visualization of over one million nodes and links. v3 moved to luma.gl on WebGL2. Notably it exposes
`start/stop/pause/unpause` — simulation is decoupled from rendering by design.

deck.gl: ~60 fps panning and zooming up to ~1M data items, dropping to 10–20 fps near 10M, and crashing
somewhere between 10M and 100M on Chrome's 1 GB single-allocation cap.

Sigma.js: 100k edges fine with default styles, but struggles at 5k nodes *with icons* — the cost is
per-item visual detail, not item count. That is the argument for zoom-band LOD stated as a source.

- https://openjsf.org/blog/introducing-cosmos-gl
- https://github.com/cosmosgl/graph
- https://deck.gl/docs/developer-guide/performance
- https://github.com/jacomyal/sigma.js/issues/239

### Rive / Skia / GPUI

Rive's renderer is open source and reduces antialiased vector paths to triangle patches to ride the GPU's
rasterization pipeline; positioned as Skia-like but for newer GPU architectures. Skia moves *some* work
to the GPU rather than being a pure GPU renderer. Zed's GPUI rasterizes glyphs into a GPU atlas once and
redraws them as textured quads every frame — the same trick as the symbol-atlas idea in our proposal —
and is currently replacing its `blade` backend with `wgpu`.

- https://rive.app/blog/rive-renderer-now-open-source-and-available-on-all-platforms
- https://zed.dev/blog/videogame
- https://github.com/zed-industries/zed/pull/46758

### Semantic zoom / LOD writing 2025-2026

Thin, and academic rather than industrial: semantic zoom for software cities (arXiv 2510.00003),
ZoomTable for hierarchical tables, a Codrops piece on streaming an infinite canvas around a moving
viewpoint. Nothing that supersedes spatial index + culling + zoom bands. No change to our plan.

- https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00003v1
- https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/01/07/infinite-canvas-building-a-seamless-pan-anywhere-image-space/

### WebGPU status

W3C candidate recommendation March 2026; Chrome 113+, Firefox 147+, Safari 26+; secondary sources put
global support near 85%. Roughly 45% of older devices lack storage buffers in vertex shaders and fall
back to a reduced compatibility mode. Treat the percentages as soft — several of those pages are SEO
content; the authoritative tracker is the gpuweb implementation-status wiki.

- https://github.com/gpuweb/gpuweb/wiki/Implementation-Status

## B — Simulation and canvas products

### Data center digital twins

Cadence Reality DC pairs a detailed 3D twin with a CFD solver, connected live to BMS and DCIM feeds.
High-fidelity CFD still takes hours per deployment scenario; the 2025-2026 move is AI surrogate models
trained on those runs to infer thermal behavior in seconds, plus CUDA/Omniverse acceleration (Cadence
cite up to 50x on Blackwell). Schneider Electric and ETAP built a grid-to-chip power twin on Omniverse.
This is a 3D engine over an offline or surrogate-accelerated physics solver. It is not an editable
port-level diagram, and nothing in the material suggests one.

- https://www.cadence.com/en_US/home/tools/reality-digital-twin.html
- https://www.cadence.com/en_US/home/explore/ai-factory.html
- https://semiengineering.com/data-center-digital-twins-how-simulation-improves-design-and-performance/

### Browser circuit simulators

CircuitVerse is a canvas-based digital-logic editor with real-time simulation aimed at classrooms;
Falstad's CircuitJS1 is a GWT port of the old Java applet. No published component ceiling for either.
The nearest evidence is a CircuitJS1 issue reporting the JS version became resource-heavy and
"generally unusable"; DigiSim's free tier caps at 30 components, which is a product decision not a limit.
**Correction to the previous session:** "browser circuit simulators die at a few thousand parts" is
plausible but I could not source a number. It should be measured, not asserted.

- https://deepwiki.com/CircuitVerse/CircuitVerse/5-circuit-simulator
- https://github.com/sharpie7/circuitjs1/issues/41

### "Figma + logic" canvases with live data

Cloudcraft (now Datadog) generates live AWS diagrams from real resource data and paints overlays on them
— e.g. security misconfigurations in context. Lucidscale imports AWS/Azure/GCP to auto-generate diagrams
and inventories while letting you add shapes manually. Isoflow embeds live interactive topology diagrams.
All three are inventory visualization with data overlays. None simulates anything, and all are scoped to
a cloud account's resource count, not millions of ports.

Rive's state machines are a node canvas driving live runtime behavior, but the scale is UI artboards.
Simulink co-simulates with Unreal in lock-step; Simulink Online does not support that co-simulation at all.

- https://blog.cloudcraft.co/produce-clear-easy-to-understand-diagrams-instantly-with-cloudcrafts-new-live-experience/
- https://lucid.co/lucidscale
- https://isoflow.io/
- https://help.rive.app/runtimes/state-machines
- https://www.mathworks.com/help/driving/ug/unreal-engine-simulation-environment-requirements-and-limitations.html

### WebGPU compute simulation in the browser

Documented examples: 250,000 particles simulated and drawn entirely on the GPU with the render pass
reading the same buffer the compute pass wrote; a Particle Life implementation; N-body with shared-memory
tiling. Claims of 1M interactive particles at 60 fps appear in blog-grade sources, and the reason given is
the one that matters to us: the data never leaves the GPU between compute and draw.
Evidence quality is demos and blog posts, not benchmarks.

- https://lisyarus.github.io/blog/posts/particle-life-simulation-in-browser-using-webgpu.html
- https://medium.com/@dev48v/webgpu-i-simulated-250-000-particles-entirely-on-the-gpu-in-the-browser-e5c64eb61c4f

### "Simulation engine + spatial index + renderer as separate layers"

This architecture is written up in 2025-2026 digital-twin research — a simulation engine for component
behavior with instances rendered separately, voxel Spatial IDs as a unified multi-resolution spatial
index, fVDB for large 3D spaces — but always geospatial/3D, never diagram tools. No published
architecture writing covers our shape.

- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mice.70146
- https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-23943-7

## Verdict on the previous session's unsourced claim

Substantially confirmed, with two corrections.

Confirmed: DC digital twins are 3D engines over offline or surrogate-accelerated physics, not editable
canvases. Infinite-canvas tools solve rendering and LOD with no simulation. Cloud diagram tools do live
data overlays but no simulation and no port-level scale. Nothing found does all three at datacenter scale
as an editable diagram.

Correction 1 — cosmos.gl is closer prior art than the last session allowed. It runs a real simulation
(force layout) and rendering over 1M+ entities in a browser on WebGL2, with the two decoupled. It has no
domain model and is not editable, so the gap stands, but "nobody simulates and draws a million entities
in a browser" is wrong. The engineering is proven; the product combination is what is unbuilt.

Correction 2 — the "circuit simulators die at a few thousand parts" figure has no source. Drop it or
measure it.

## What this changes in the recommendation

The three-step plan holds. Two adjustments.

1. Design the render layer with a WebGPU path, not WebGL2 only. WebGPU reached candidate recommendation
   in March 2026 and Figma shipped their production renderer on it in September 2025 with a runtime
   fallback to WebGL. Compute shaders are what let dynamics (temperature, voltage, payload) run over the
   awake set without a CPU round trip, which is exactly the overlay problem. Follow Figma: WebGPU first,
   WebGL2 fallback, one shader source.
2. Vello stays off the table while it is alpha and Firefox/Safari WebGPU is experimental in their own docs.

Nothing in the research argues against the Rust core with `bevy_ecs`, and nothing makes it more urgent
than step 1. Figma is the counter-example worth weighing: one compiled core in WASM, no socket, no
second process. The socket design earns its cost from the local-program and hosting requirement, not
from rendering. Step 1 — structure-of-arrays scene, GPU render layer, signal-engine and routing index
fixes — is unchanged, still gated by `tests/scale_benchmark.py`, and still needed under every option.
